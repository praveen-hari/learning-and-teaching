1:08 Rote Learning and Meaningful

Learning

Rote learning is reproduction of the learned
material verbatim. The learner is not interested
in checking whether the material learned by him
is correct or incorrect. On the other hand in
meaningful learning, the learners understand
and internalize the matter they learn. So they
do not try to reproduce the matter learned as
such but try to express it in their own language.
In other words, the output of the meaningful
learner will be much more than the input they
received.

The rote learners function fully depending
on the teacher. But those who learn meaningfully

Teaching and Learning and their Nature 19

have the ability to function independently as they
try to understand and analyze anything they
learn. Rote learners could be compared to
parasites and those who learn meaningfully to
lush green plants. Parasites are incapable of
giving fruits while plants and trees grow
independently and flourish, having the capacity
to bear fruits.

Those who learn by rote memory will express
only few particular responses. On the other hand
those who learn meaningfully will display newer
responses too. Rote learners will repeat like
parrots what they had been taught. On the other
hand, those who learn with meaning will enrich
what have been taught by adding their
imagination and present them in a novel way.

While rote learning is a passive activity, in
meaningful learning, the learners function
actively and get fully involved. ;

1:10 Principles of Active Learning

The following are the five important

principles of Active Learning.

1. Learning involves the active construction

of meaning by the learner. Learning is the
process, in which the learner on receiving a
new information, examines it in the light of
information that he already possess and
makes it meaningful. If the already existing
information is completely contradicting the
new information received, then the already
existing information is removed from the mind
and thus forming a new conceptualization. In
brief, learning is that which helps in the
construction of cognitive structure.

Learning facts and learning to do
something are two different activities. This
explains why students can know a set of facts
and still be unable to apply those facts to solve
a problem. If students are to successfully use
knowledge, they must have opportunities to
practise that and obtain feedback. A variety

Teaching and Learning and their Nature 25

of other instructional techniques follow from
this principle. For acquiring the ability to solve
problems, the learner need to know more than

to what type of situations. The sequence of
problems from easy to hard is also important
while giving assignments to students.
Students should move to harder problems only
after being able to solve the easy ones. On
the other hand, if students are required to

solve difficult problems at the beginning itself,

then that will affect their learning.

Some things that are learned are specific
toa particular domain or context whereas
