3:05 -Nature of Learning Process as per
the Constructivist Theory

According to the constructivist theory,
construction of knowledge takes place in short term
memory. (This is also known as working memory
of the learners). In this process, the learner uses
the new information obtained from his environment
and the knowledge already stored in his long term
memory i.e. to meaningfully interpret the new
information received; he selects the appropriate
ones from the knowledge in store of his memory
and compares with them. Then he organizes the
new information and integrates them with his
knowledge pool. :

There are three important activities in the
process of learning. They are: | i

i) Selecting the most appropriate from among
the information received: selecting appropriate

information from the prior knowledge stored
in memory in the form of audio and video
images; then comparing the new information
selected from among the incoming ones,
with those drawn from the prior knowledge
and interpreting. In this step two acts viz.
‘selecting’ and ‘meaningfully interpreting’
take place.

Classifying and organizing the new

information (coded in the form of audio and
video images) take place in this step.

Integrating the new information which has
been well organized (i.e. new knowledge)
with already stored knowledge in memory
and modifying the ‘cognitive structure’ take
place in this step.

1. Developing knowledge base in students:
What a person already knows, serves as the ‘data
base’ for acquiring further knowledge. It largely
determines what new information he attends to,
how he organizes and represents new information,
and how he filters new experiences, and even what
he determines to be important or relevant. So, to
create knowledge base among students, teachers
adopt student-centered teaching. (Alexander &
Murphy, 2000).

2.Developing strategic processing and
executive control of one’s thoughts and
behaviours: The ability to reflect on and regulate
one’s thoughts and behaviours is an essential
aspect of learning. Successful students actively
involve themselves in their learning i.e. monitor
their thinking, reflect on their learning, and assume
themselves responsible for their learning:
(Lambert & McCombs, 2000). Teachers who try
to develop the above referred qualities in students,
which help to promote successful Jearning, adopt
student-centered teaching.

Plato was born in Athens ot the ancient country

the formation of a noble character. Like Socrates,
Plato too used dialogue and debate in carrying out
philosophical enquiries in his attempt to find the

epistemological truth.

Sense perception does not reveal the true
reality of things,. but gives us mere appearance.
Opinions developed from perceptions may be true

teaching. Putting out suitable questions to make

Questioning, discussion, logical arguments,
